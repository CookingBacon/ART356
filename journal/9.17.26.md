# Chapter 2 - The Psychology of Everyday Actions
So chapter 2 was labelled "The Psychology of Everyday Actions" and there was quite a bit of topics that Don Norman jumped between. 
To summarize, I think the chapter talked about how good design requires the designer to be able to understand how people will think, act and respond when it comes to using a product.
We talked about the gulf of execution which is the bridge between what a person is trying to do and them figuring out how to do it. On the other hand, we have the gulf of evaluation which is
the bridge between performing an action and registering whether it did the desired result or not. So the consensus between these two gulfs is that good design must cross the bridge that is the both of these gulfs
in terms of signifiers, feedback and the topics we talked about earlier in Chapter 1

There was also the seven stages of action being:
- Form a goal
- Plan
- Specify
- Perform
- Perceive
- Interpret
- Compare
- 
These seven stages say that designers should be able to design around these seven stages when making a product
There was also a section where he talked about how humans think subconsciously more than they do on-purpose which makes sense. I know I for sure go on auto-pilot when it comes to driving or my work sometimes. 
Then Norman brings up emotions and how they correlate with design in terms of visceral, behavioral and reflective. Another section he mentioned how people are always trying to explain why things happen. And then another one where he mentioned that people usually assume that they are the problem when something is difficult to use, but this is usually just due to poor design. Overall, I think this was a much more interesting read than the last chapter because I think it went a bit more into psychology. How the brain works is pretty interesting to me usually.
