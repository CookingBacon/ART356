# Chapter 4 - Knowing What to Do (Part 1)
The first part of chapter 4 was focused on constraints and how they can be useful to a design's user. I enjoyed this chapter as typically people tend to think that freedom is everything but constraints do so much in guiding you on what is actually possible. Without constraints, you'd be completely lost in how to use an object unless you had a manual or information prior to using it. Norman makes a very clear example on the usage of constraints in design with the Lego motorcycle on page 124. The four constraints mentioned are physical, cultural, semantic and logical. I was very interested in reading how perception of normality dictates your decisions and what is determined to be constraints. Free will allows you to put words upside-down or backwards but social norms tell you to put it upright and correctly faced thus creating a constraint

The other section mentioned in this part of chapter 4 was affordances and signifiers in everyday objects. These two terms were defined in a previous chapter, specifically affordances are indications of interactions on an object and signifiers are visuals that make those interactables clearer. There was a notable example of these two poorly demonstrated on page 136 with the light switches. I think partly this image of light switches was notable because of the demonstration in-class of having three light switches but the class was unsure which switch would turn off a specific set of lights. I also remember the teacher drawing a visual of their apartment and asking the class which light switch corresponds to which set of lights based on the layout. I think this particular section of the reading was memorable due to us reviewing it physically in-class. Back to page 136 though, I think the image speaks for itself. How are you supposed to know which switch that appears identical to each other, corresponds to which electronic when there's eight of them in a row? 

This reading was very interesting to me with how it made me think on societal perceptions of objects. This is how I feel while reading part 1 of Chapter 4
